- Current reason copy such as “This is the next unfinished part of the current course sequence. StudyGrid is recommending it, not calling it mastered.” is unacceptable learner-facing language.

- Remove “retrieval”, “evidence profile”, “mastered”, “later memory changes”, and similar internal engine/learning-science terms from normal learner UI unless absolutely necessary and explained.

- “0 later memory changes in later session” is unintelligible and must be replaced with a human concept such as “Not checked again yet” / “No follow-up check yet” if that metric is useful at all.

QUESTION / ADAPTIVE ENGINE DIRECTION
- Questions must not be static repeated quiz items.
- Early questions may be easier while StudyGrid establishes what the learner understands.
- As learner evidence improves, questions should get harder and require finer discrimination/application.
- Questions should vary/adapt based on the source material, prior responses, misconceptions, support/hints used and demonstrated performance; do not simply recycle the same prompt/options.
- Strong multiple choice often means A/B/C/D all look plausible without domain knowledge; distractors are nearby concepts, conditions, exceptions or realistic misconceptions.
- Do not use absurd/irrelevant distractors.
- Build this as StudyGrid Engine behaviour/quality contract, not “ask generic AI to make another quiz”.
- Beta can start with bounded deterministic item families/difficulty/misconception tags before more advanced psychometrics if needed, but architecture must not prevent later adaptive testing/item-response approaches.

GENERAL / NON-CLASS LEARNING BUSINESS DIRECTION

- Examples: job training, CPR, pilot licence/certification, professional learning, personal topics.
- Longer-term opportunity: external training/course providers could host/approve course material on StudyGrid and offer StudyGrid as a learning-support layer; commercial structure could include revenue sharing. Treat this as product/business direction, not approved implementation scope yet.
