AMEND BEFORE BUILD
A. EASING / OVERSHOOT
- Do not lock in Gemini's spring example cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1). It may feel playful/bouncy rather than premium/mechanical and can amplify motion. Default direction is a damped snap-and-settle with little or no visible overshoot. If any settle exists, keep it extremely small and validate visually.

B. ROTATION NUMBERS
- Do not hardcode 90–120deg or -35deg/+120deg as the contract yet. Those are useful prototype seeds, not accepted geometry. The final angles should be derived from the chosen three-position spatial layout and shortest coherent path, then tuned for legibility and perceived physical continuity.

C. BLUR
- Treat blur as optional/minimal. CSS filters on transformed text can become visually soft or expensive, especially in Safari/iPad-class rendering. Prefer depth, opacity and perspective first; use only a very small transient blur if testing proves it improves depth without harming crispness/performance.

D. ACCESSIBILITY STRUCTURE
- Do not aria-hide an entire interactive component if it contains usable controls. If the rotor itself is decorative, hide only the visual moving text from AT and provide a stable semantic summary/list plus separately accessible controls where manual advance/pause exists.
- For prefers-reduced-motion, default to static/instantaneous state change or a very subtle non-spatial transition. Do not assume crossfade is always appropriate for users asking for reduced motion.

E. INTERACTION
- Do not make the active phrase a clickable navigation target merely because Gemini suggested click-to-advance. The phrase is explanatory content unless owner/product intent assigns an action. Manual advance, if needed, should have clear semantics and not add clutter. Research should weigh autoplay-only, pause control, subtle dots, or tap-to-advance.

F. RETURNING USER ADAPTATION
