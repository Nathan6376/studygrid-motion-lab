1. PRODUCT-PROCESS RECOMMENDATION FOR STUDYGRID
======================================================================

EXTERNAL EVIDENCE
- ISO 9241-210:2019 specifies human-centred design principles/activities across the lifecycle of interactive systems; ISO reports the edition was reviewed and confirmed in 2025 and remains current.
- Design Council's Double Diamond explicitly alternates divergence and convergence: Discover user/problem evidence, Define the challenge, Develop multiple potential solutions, Deliver by testing/selecting/refining. Design Council also states the process is iterative rather than strictly linear.

STUDYGRID DESIGN JUDGEMENT — SMALLEST RIGOROUS PROCESS
Use Nathan's required method as a compact evidence-gated Double Diamond/UCD loop:

GATE 0 — PURPOSE CONTRACT
For each material surface/change, write one short contract before design:
- Who is acting, and in what role/context?
- What job/problem are they trying to complete?
- What must they understand, do next, and know is complete?
- What current evidence says the experience is failing?
- What success/failure would be observable in a prototype/QA pass?

GATE 1 — CURRENT-STATE + EVIDENCE PASS
Before visual design:
- pin exact candidate/source behaviour;
- map current route/state/history/focus semantics;
- identify role/data/learning constraints;
- identify accessibility and motion non-negotiables;
- check platform feasibility only far enough to rule out impossible designs.

GATE 2 — DIVERGE
Produce at least 2 materially different interaction concepts for a material UI decision. Variants that only change colour/radius/spacing do not count as meaningful divergence.

GATE 3 — COMPARE / SELECT
Compare concepts against:
1. purpose/task clarity;
2. orientation/wayfinding;
3. target stability and interaction cost;
4. accessibility/reduced-motion behaviour;
5. learner/instructor role coherence;
6. technical feasibility/fallback risk;
7. owner design direction and existing visual language.
Record why the selected/reconciled concept is stronger; do not jump from research to implementation by default.

GATE 4 — INTERACTIVE PROTOTYPE + TASK CHECK
Prototype the critical flow using realistic content/state. Check whether a user can answer the five owner questions:
- Where am I?
- What am I doing?
- Why am I doing it?
- What happens next?
- What have I completed?
If the model/task remains unclear, return to Define/Develop rather than polishing pixels.

GATE 5 — BOUNDED IMPLEMENTATION
Implement the selected concept as one coherent package on the exact accepted base. Do not accumulate unrelated local patches.

GATE 6 — INDEPENDENT QA / ACCEPTANCE
Independently verify route/history, focus, keyboard/touch/iPad behaviour, reduced motion, layout stability, semantics, learning-state correctness, regression criteria and exact owner acceptance conditions.

WHAT MUST EXIST BEFORE MATERIAL BUILD
Required before implementation:
- purpose/task contract;
- exact current-source behaviour;
- interaction/IA semantics and role boundaries;
- relevant accessibility/motion constraints;
- at least 2 meaningful concepts and a selection rationale;
- a feasibility/fallback check for any browser/API dependency that materially changes the concept.

CAN ITERATE AFTER PROTOTYPE WITHOUT REOPENING THE WHOLE DESIGN
Usually safe to tune after the selected interaction model works:
- local spacing and type scale;
- microcopy precision that does not change the job/state meaning;
- easing/duration within the agreed motion contract;
- visual polish such as restrained gloss/border treatment;
- non-semantic icon/ornament details.
If iteration changes route meaning, hierarchy, role context, state model, focus order or learning workflow, it is not mere polish and should return to the relevant earlier gate.

2. IA / WAYFINDING PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

EXTERNAL EVIDENCE
- WAI page-structure guidance emphasizes meaningful regions/headings for orientation and navigation.
- WCAG Page Titled requires descriptive titles; W3C explicitly notes that SPA titles should update to reflect the current view.
- Breadcrumb guidance from multiple public design systems treats breadcrumbs as hierarchy/location, not browser-history replay; they are most useful in genuinely multi-level structures and unnecessary in flat/linear flows.
- Choice-overload meta-analysis (Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman, 2015; 99 observations, N=7,202) finds overload is conditional, especially on choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty and decision goals. Therefore "fewer choices is always better" is not evidence-based; the goal is fewer simultaneous ambiguous choices at difficult moments.

STUDYGRID DESIGN JUDGEMENT
A. AUTHENTICATED ROOT
- Establish one durable signed-in Home/current-workspace root.
- Completing first-start/onboarding should not leave a pseudo-Back path into onboarding as if onboarding were a parent workspace.
- Account exit is Sign out, not Back.
- "Your Classes" should exist as a destination only if it owns a real recurring job (browse/manage classes). If it is only an empty transition to another choice, fold that choice into Home or a scoped class selector.

B. FOUR NAVIGATION LAYERS — DO NOT DUPLICATE THEM
1. Global/account: Home, profile/settings, explicit role/context access.
2. Role/workspace: learner vs instructor/admin context when the account has multiple capabilities.
3. Content context: selected class or self-directed/personal study context.
4. Local task/mode: overview/lesson/practice/review or instructor-local destinations.
A control should belong primarily to one layer. Repeating the same course identity and destinations across top bar + rail + page header creates chrome without adding orientation.

C. CLASS-BASED + SELF-DIRECTED STUDY
Keep the product class-centred without making classes mandatory for every learning task by making the root mental model "current study/workspace" rather than "class list".
Recommended Home structure:
- PRIMARY: one contextual Continue/Next recommendation with its class/personal-study context visible;
- SECONDARY: browse/switch class;
- SECONDARY: study something outside a class / personal study;
- tertiary setup/history/settings as appropriate.
This keeps one start point while preserving exploration.

D. ROLE CONTEXT
Instructor, learner and personal study are not just tabs in one flat destination set. The shell must make the current role/workspace explicit. Entering a study surface from instructor context must either:
- remain clearly an instructor preview/review of material; or
- deliberately transition into a learner/study context with a visible way back.
Do not silently switch role meaning because the user clicked "Study".

E. BACK / UP / HOME
- Browser/history Back: returns through meaningful prior navigation state.
- Hierarchy Up: goes to semantic parent, independently of how the user arrived.
- Home: explicit durable root.
- Breadcrumbs: hierarchy/location only when hierarchy is deep enough to help.
Do not label an explicit parent destination as generic Back if it does not replay meaningful prior context.

F. SAME-LEVEL MODES
Learn/Test/Support or equivalent peer modes should use a stable shared shell and behave like same-level panels/tabs where feasible:
- persistent controls stay put;
- the active state is explicit;
- the content region changes without moving the mode controls;
- keyboard focus remains predictable.
WAI APG tabs provide the established tab/tabpanel keyboard and aria-selected model when the semantics genuinely are tabs.

G. RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP WITHOUT LOCK-IN
At key entry/completion points, show one strongest recommendation with the reason/context, then lower-emphasis alternatives. Avoid presenting Learn + Notes + Questions + Browse Lessons + Quick + Focus + Deep as equally weighted starting decisions unless they genuinely represent distinct jobs.

3. MOTION / TRANSITION PATTERN MATRIX
======================================================================

EXTERNAL PLATFORM STATE — CURRENT AS OF 2026-10-01
- MDN marks ViewTransition as Baseline 2025 / available across latest browsers since Oct 2025, with some feature-support variation.
- Safari 18.2 shipped cross-document View Transitions; Safari 18.0 had same-document support.
- MDN marks Navigation API Baseline 2026 / latest-browser availability since Jan 2026, with older-browser gaps.
- Web Animations API is widely available; CSS/WAAPI remain suitable for component-local animation.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is widely available and communicates a user preference to remove/reduce/replace nonessential motion.
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 requires pause/stop/hide for automatically moving/blinking/scrolling content that lasts >5 seconds and is presented alongside other content, unless essential.

PATTERN MATRIX — STUDYGRID DESIGN JUDGEMENT

RELATIONSHIP: PARENT -> CHILD / ENTERING DEEPER CONTEXT
Preferred motion: shared-element/container continuity or restrained directional movement while keeping persistent shell fixed.
Why: communicates hierarchy and preserves where the new content came from.
Reject: whole-screen theatrical slide that moves fixed navigation, delays input or creates false physical hierarchy.

RELATIONSHIP: CHILD -> PARENT
Preferred motion: inverse/reverse relationship when useful, or a simple continuity transition.
Reject: unrelated animation direction that breaks the spatial model.

RELATIONSHIP: SIBLING / SAME-LEVEL MODE
Preferred motion: quiet crossfade or very short low-amplitude horizontal/opacity transition; shared controls stay fixed.
Why: peers are not deeper/shallower destinations.
Reject: moving the Learn/Test/Support controls, full-page zoom, or layout reflow that makes the user reacquire the target.

RELATIONSHIP: LOCAL REVEAL / POPOVER / HELP
Preferred motion: anchored fade/scale or short reveal that keeps its source visually connected.
Reject: unrelated page movement or content jump.

RELATIONSHIP: COMPLETION / STATE CHANGE
Preferred motion: local settle/check/progress update; then stable forward next-action surface.
Reject: celebratory animation that becomes the task or sends the user visually backward.

IMPLEMENTATION INFERENCE
- Route correctness/history/state must work without animation.
- View Transition API is a progressive-enhancement candidate for route/view continuity, not a routing system.
- Navigation API can simplify modern same-origin history/navigation handling but is newly Baseline 2026; StudyGrid should feature-detect and preserve a fallback rather than make navigation correctness depend on it.
- CSS/WAAPI are appropriate for local component motion and the welcome loop; animations should be cancellable and state-driven.

WELCOME 1 -> 2 -> 3 CONTINUOUS LOOP — THREE RESEARCH CANDIDATES

CANDIDATE A — SHARED-AXIS ORDERED SLIDE [strongest sequence candidate]
- Fixed stage/orb/loop anchor.
- Current numbered step exits a short distance along one consistent axis; next step enters from the same sequence direction.
- Copy stays readable at rest; CTA remains outside the moving region.
Strength: most directly communicates ordered progression 1→2→3→1.
Risk: continuous translation can become distracting if amplitude/duration is too large.

CANDIDATE B — STABLE-STAGE CROSSFADE / SHARED NUMBER MORPH [quietest candidate]
- Stage geometry does not move.
- Number/progress marker and copy crossfade/morph locally; stable StudyGrid loop/orb can subtly settle.
Strength: minimal orientation cost; naturally close to reduced-motion treatment.
Risk: weaker sense of physical sequence unless numbering/progress cue is strong.

CANDIDATE C — RESTRAINED CYCLIC ROTOR / CARD TURN [brand-forward experimental candidate]
- 1/2/3 rotate around one implied compact axis; only one message is fully front-facing/readable at a time.
- Motion must be shallow enough that text is not read while angled/blurred.
Strength: strongest loop metaphor and continuity with StudyGrid's existing motion language.
Risk: highest distraction/legibility/vestibular cost; can feel gimmicky if used beyond the hero.

AUTOPLAY CONTRACT
Nathan's current requirement is continuous automatic cycling; that can coexist with accessibility only if user control is real:
- Provide a persistent Pause/Play or equivalent control if continuous movement runs >5 seconds alongside other content.
- Do not rely only on "pause while focused" as the sole user control.
- User interaction with a step should pause the cycle so the interface does not fight reading/exploration; explicit Resume may restart it.
- Keep interactive CTA/button targets outside the moving sequence region where possible.
- Do not move a focused control out from under the user.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: remove large translation/rotation; use static/manual step state or near-instant/low-motion content replacement.
- Dwell and transition duration are prototype variables, not standards. A reasonable initial test hypothesis is roughly 3–5 seconds of readable dwell and a short ~200–350 ms transition, then user-test with the actual copy. Do not treat those numbers as evidence-backed universal values.

4. STUDY SESSION / LEARNING FLOW
======================================================================

EXTERNAL EVIDENCE
- Retrieval-practice reviews show that retrieving learned material strengthens later retention across many materials/learner groups; corrective feedback further helps error correction and retention.
- A 2025 systematic/meta-analytic review comparing retrieval practice with elaborative encoding found only a small overall retrieval advantage, but the advantage was materially conditional on feedback; when feedback was absent, elaborative approaches could outperform retrieval in those comparisons. This argues against StudyGrid behaving like a context-free quiz dump.
- Confidence/metacognition research shows confidence is useful but inferential/imperfect. Q-R3 already established the StudyGrid evidence contract: `answer -> optional confidence -> correctness/correct answer/explanation -> adaptive update`.
- Choice overload evidence says difficult, complex and preference-uncertain decisions are where extra options are more likely to hurt.

STUDYGRID DESIGN JUDGEMENT — ONE COHERENT LEARNING LOOP
A StudyGrid study session should make the mode/state legible rather than mixing all evidence at once:
1. TEACH / ORIENT — short explanation, worked example, source context or lesson segment when the learner lacks the basis for retrieval.
2. RETRIEVE — one clear question/task that requires recall/application rather than rereading.
3. CONFIDENCE — optional pre-feedback judgement when enabled, before correctness is revealed.
4. FEEDBACK — clear right/wrong + correct answer/explanation; avoid an overloaded metadata wall.
5. ERROR REVIEW — explain the misconception/context needed for missed or high-confidence-wrong responses; let the learner retry later rather than immediately declaring mastery.
6. PROGRESS — update what changed in the session/model, with reward/progress as secondary feedback.
7. NEXT — one recommended next action; alternatives remain available but subordinate.

TEACHING VS QUIZZING
If a learner has not been introduced to the material in the current context, StudyGrid should not present a sequence of unexplained retrieval questions and call that the whole learning experience. Retrieval remains valuable, but explanation/source context and corrective feedback are part of the learning loop.

STABLE REPETITIVE TARGETS
For workflows such as 15-question instructor review or repeated learner practice:
- keep Next / Approve and next / confidence controls in stable locations where the layout permits;
- swap content inside a stable frame instead of moving the primary action after each item;
- terminal item must change to an explicit completion/finalisation state rather than leaving an inert `Approve and next` button.
This is a StudyGrid interaction judgement informed by predictable focus/target principles; it is not a separate WCAG numeric rule.

QUICK / FOCUSED / DEEP — KEEP ONLY IF THE JOBS DIFFER
There is no evidence-backed universal three-tier study taxonomy. The labels are defensible only if each maps to a meaningfully different plan and outcome.

TESTABLE PRODUCT HYPOTHESES, NOT RESEARCHED UNIVERSAL DURATIONS:
- Quick review: ~5 min; retrieve due/recent items; minimal setup; finish a small bounded set.
- Focus session: ~15–20 min; one selected topic/skill; teach -> retrieve -> error review -> checkpoint.
- Deep session: ~30–45 min; broader module/multiple linked concepts; explanation/worked material + retrieval + error review + progress summary.
If the scheduler/content plan does not actually change in these ways, remove or collapse the labels. A stronger default may be `Continue` plus an optional `Choose session` path.

END-OF-SESSION CONTRACT
Completion should feel forward-moving:
- state accomplishment first (`Session complete`, what was practised/reviewed);
- summarize what changed (`3 strong`, `2 to revisit`, etc., only if real state supports it);
- give one recommended next action;
- retain navigation such as Back to course/Progress as secondary options, not the emotional main action.

5. ACCESSIBILITY NON-NEGOTIABLES
======================================================================

FOCUS
- Never remove visible keyboard focus to solve the sharp green-square complaint.
- Use `:focus-visible`/equivalent input-aware styling so pointer activation need not look like persistent keyboard focus when the platform would not normally show it.
- Focus visual must be visually distinct from selected/active/tutorial-target state.
- WCAG 2.4.7 requires visible focus at AA. WCAG 2.4.13 Focus Appearance is AAA and can be used as a stronger internal design target (area roughly equivalent to a 2 CSS px perimeter with 3:1 change contrast); do not misstate it as an AA minimum.
- WCAG 2.4.11 AA requires focused components not be entirely obscured by author-created content.

FOCUS STYLE DESIGN JUDGEMENT
Replace the current hard square with an on-brand indicator that remains unmistakable, e.g. rounded outer ring/halo or edge treatment with adequate contrast and offset. Do not use the same StudyGrid green fill/border language for focus, selection and success simultaneously.

ROUTE / SPA ORIENTATION
- Update `document.title` for distinct SPA views; W3C explicitly calls this out for dynamic SPA pages.
- Keep meaningful main landmarks/headings and labelled navigation regions.
- For a true page/route context change, deliberately manage focus so keyboard/screen-reader users are oriented to the new content; do not leave focus in removed DOM.
- For same-level tab/mode change, follow tab semantics: focus remains on/within the tab interaction model and the corresponding panel updates; do not jump focus arbitrarily into content.
- Status-only updates such as completion/progress messages that do not change context should be exposed programmatically without stealing focus.

MOTION
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing/replacing nonessential spatial motion.
- Continuous welcome motion needs pause/stop/hide control under WCAG 2.2.2 when it meets the criterion's autoplay/duration/parallel-content conditions.
- Route function and learning state must remain correct when animation is skipped/cancelled.

TARGETS / REPETITIVE WORK
- WCAG 2.5.8 AA requires pointer targets at least 24x24 CSS px or one of its spacing/equivalent/inline/UA/essential exceptions.
- Stable placement for repeated actions is an additional usability target, especially for fast review loops.

MULTI-SELECT
- For ordinary quiz answers, prefer native semantic checkbox groups (`fieldset`/`legend` + checkboxes) when the interaction is simply choosing multiple independent answers.
- If a custom listbox is genuinely needed, WAI APG requires the proper multi-select model (`aria-multiselectable=true`, consistent selected states and keyboard behaviour). Do not fake multi-select visually while only one value can be selected.

7. QUESTIONS THE DESIGNS MUST ANSWER — ASTRA / BOB CHECKLIST
======================================================================

Before a concept is selected or built, it should answer these plainly:

PURPOSE / ENTRY
1. What exact job is this surface doing that the prior/parent surface does not?
2. On entry, can the user tell where they are, their role/context, and the strongest next action?
3. Does this surface deserve to be a destination, or is it only a transition/control that should live in Home/context UI?

NAVIGATION / IA
4. Is this control global, role-level, class-level, or local-task navigation — and is it duplicated elsewhere?
5. What should Back do here: history, parent, or explicit destination? Is that meaning reflected in the label?
6. Can a signed-in user accidentally Back into onboarding/first-start?
7. Can class study and personal/general study coexist without presenting two competing product mental models?
8. When role/context changes, is the transition explicit and reversible?

STUDY FLOW
9. Is the user being taught/explained enough before retrieval when needed?
10. Are retrieval, confidence, feedback/error correction and progress states visually and semantically distinct?
11. Is there one recommended next step instead of an undifferentiated menu of study entry points?
12. If Quick/Focus/Deep remain, what distinct job, time expectation, content plan and completion output does each own?
13. Does the last item in a repeated workflow produce an explicit terminal state and forward next action?

STABILITY / MOTION
14. Which relationship is being communicated: parent-child, sibling, local reveal, or completion? Does the motion match that relationship?
15. Which controls/anchors must remain spatially stable while content changes?
16. Can the animation be skipped/cancelled without breaking state/history/focus?
17. For the continuous 1→2→3 loop, where is Pause/Play and what happens under reduced motion or direct user interaction?

ACCESSIBILITY
18. Is keyboard focus always visible, distinguishable from selection/success, and not obscured?
19. Does a same-level mode use predictable tab/panel semantics where appropriate?
20. Are multi-select questions actually multi-select with semantic state/keyboard/touch support?
21. Are dynamic route titles/headings/landmarks updated so assistive-technology users can orient to the new view?
22. Are touch/pointer targets and repeated actions usable without chasing moving controls?

IMPLEMENTATION / QA
23. Does the concept depend on View Transition/Navigation APIs, and what is the correct fallback?
24. What exact current-source selectors/state/routes will change, and what must remain byte/behaviourally untouched?
25. What independent acceptance evidence proves the purpose was achieved, not merely that the UI changed?
