Material directions established:
1. Engine v1 should be a hybrid evidence engine, not a single-model product. Separate course/skill graph, learner evidence state, memory/retention state, assessment evidence, deterministic safety/eligibility rules, and next-action policy.
2. BKT/PFA/IRT/CDM families remain strong interpretable baselines; deep KT can improve prediction on some datasets but demands more data, is harder to interpret/calibrate, and should not be the v1 decision authority merely because AUC is higher.
3. Prediction accuracy is not causal evidence that a recommendation improves learning. Next-action policy needs explicit pedagogic constraints and later experimentation; contextual-bandit/RL approaches belong behind a validated baseline rather than at cold start.
4. Retrieval practice + distributed practice have strong evidence; interleaving is conditional; worked examples/fading help especially for novices; feedback quality/timing matters. A StudyGrid engine must choose teaching interventions as well as questions.
5. FSRS/DSR-style memory scheduling is useful as a retention submodel, not the whole engine. Course prerequisites, assessment urgency, conceptual weakness and current instructional state must constrain/override pure flashcard due dates.
6. Interaction evidence should be ordered by validity/privacy: correctness, repeated retrieval history, source-aligned skill/item mappings, spacing/recency, explicit pre-feedback confidence, hint/retry/error pattern; response time is conditional; raw pointer/keystroke traces remain unjustified/invasive.
7. Instructor dashboards must lead from evidence to plausible action and expose uncertainty; literature repeatedly finds many dashboards stop at awareness and lack actionability/theoretical grounding.
8. ~20 students + one professor can falsify usability, comprehension, instrumentation, calibration and workflow assumptions, but cannot establish long-term efficacy or generalize adaptive-policy effects.
