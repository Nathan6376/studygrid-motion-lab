CORE DRAFT-1 CHANGE
Draft 0 collapsed too many different kinds of things into clean-sounding primitives.

Draft 1 separates them:

OBSERVATION = what actually happened.
EVIDENCE INTERPRETATION = what that observation may mean for a particular claim.
LEARNER ESTIMATE = a derived, uncertain model output.
PEDAGOGICAL NEED = a hypothesis about what may help next.
POLICY DECISION = the action StudyGrid chooses under explicit constraints.

These layers MUST remain distinguishable in data, code, explanation and audit.

======================================================================
0. PRODUCT PURPOSE
======================================================================

StudyGrid is a constrained learning decision system.

Its job is NOT to pretend it knows a mathematically unique “best” action.
Its job is to select a DEFENSIBLE NEXT LEARNING ACTION from eligible options using:
- the actual course/material structure;
- what the learner has actually done;
- explicit evidence interpretations;
- uncertainty;
- prerequisite/readiness relations;
- retention evidence where valid;
- assessment/deadline context;
- current activity/assessment rules;
- learner-declared goals or constraints;
- anti-repetition and learner-agency rules.

The system then:
1. shows one primary next action;
2. gives a simple reason;
3. allows an appropriate override;
4. records exactly why the decision happened;
5. observes what happens next;
6. updates derived estimates without rewriting history.

CORE QUESTION
“What is a defensible next action for this learner, in this course context, under these rules, based on this evidence — and why?”

This is more honest and more testable than claiming “the best thing to learn next.”

======================================================================
1. COURSE / KNOWLEDGE MODEL — DO NOT FORCE ONE UNIVERSAL ATOM
======================================================================

Draft 0’s single Learning Target primitive is SPLIT.

1A. SOURCE ARTIFACT
A versioned source document/material used by the course or learner.
Examples: syllabus, textbook chapter, lecture material, instructor handout, policy, worked solution, personal uploaded notes.

1B. CONTENT ASSERTION / PROCEDURE
A versioned proposition, rule, step, definition, relationship or procedure supported by one or more source spans.
It records:
- source/version;
- source span or provenance;
- authority class;
- course/jurisdiction/scope where relevant;
- effective/supersession state;
- approval status.

“Course truth” means canonical-for-this-course/version, not universal truth.

1C. CURRICULUM OUTCOME / COMPETENCY
An official or instructor-approved expectation about what the learner should know or be able to do.
Use stable identifiers; CASE-style interoperability may be useful later.

1D. LEARNING CLAIM
A StudyGrid-operational statement that can be supported or challenged by evidence.
Example shapes:
- distinguish X from Y under condition Z;
- solve this class of equation;
- explain principle Q;
- sequence procedure P;
- apply rule R to a novel scenario.

1E. KNOWLEDGE COMPONENT (OPTIONAL)
A model-level hypothesis used only when a learner model benefits from a finer cognitive decomposition.
A KC is NOT automatically identical to a curriculum outcome or learning claim.
It may change as evidence shows the original decomposition was poor.

1F. RELATION EDGE
Typed, versioned many-to-many relation between objects.
Possible relation types:
- supports;
- assesses;
- requires/prerequisite;
- part-of;
- related-to;
- supersedes;
- derived-from.

Prerequisite relation status must distinguish at least:
- hard/explicitly required;
- soft/hypothesized;
- informational.

No single bad inferred edge may silently become an absolute learner block.

PRODUCT LANGUAGE
“Learning Target” may remain useful in editor/UI language as an umbrella term. It is NOT the one backend atom.

======================================================================
2. TASK / ASSESSMENT CONTRACT
======================================================================

A TASK/ITEM is a versioned thing a learner is asked to do.
It carries:
- interaction type;
- response schema;
- scoring/rubric definition;
- source/content provenance;
- mapped learning claim(s)/optional KC(s);
- intended evidence role;
- task family/variant identity;
- support/feedback rules;
- assessment purpose/stakes policy references.

DOMAIN-NEUTRAL INTERACTION FAMILIES
At minimum the internal design must allow:
- single select;
- unordered multi-select;
- explicit sequence/order;
- match/pair;
- categorize;
- short/constructed response;
- numeric/formula where supported;
- hotspot/diagram where supported later.

Unsupported constructs MUST NOT silently fall back to ordinary multiple choice.

ORDERING
If order is the construct, say so explicitly and provide a clear sequencing interaction. Drag may be optional, but equivalent move-up/down/direct-position controls are required.

======================================================================
3. CLAIM → EVIDENCE → TASK CONTRACT
======================================================================

Every learning claim needs an explicit EVIDENCE CONTRACT.

Minimum contract:
- claim ID + version;
- task/item ID + version;
- intended evidence role: direct / partial / supporting / diagnostic-only;
- observed response/artifact;
- scoring/rubric + scorer/version;
- response conditions: attempt, delay, hints, examples, feedback/support already received;
- relevant accommodation/accessibility context only where necessary for validity;
- source/course version;
- task→claim/KC mapping version and rationale;
- known validity/confound flags;
- learner-estimator/inference-rule version.

WHY
A correct recognition item is not automatically evidence of application.
A coached success is not identical to unaided retrieval.
A repeated identical item can measure prompt memory rather than transferable knowledge.

======================================================================
4. IMMUTABLE OBSERVATION LEDGER
======================================================================

An OBSERVATION / ATTEMPT stores what happened, not what StudyGrid wishes it meant.

Examples:
- task presented;
- response committed;
- raw/scored result;
- rubric output;
- support/hints/examples used;
- confidence supplied before feedback, if enabled;
- teaching intervention shown;
- delayed retrieval attempt;
- instructor/assessor external observation where appropriate.

OBSERVATIONS MUST NOT CONTAIN AN INTRINSIC “MASTERY WEIGHT.”

A later model may reinterpret an old observation without rewriting the historical event.

NOT MASTERY EVIDENCE BY ITSELF
- page opened;
- lesson viewed;
- completion checkbox;
- dwell time;
- scrolling;
- streak;
- override;
- non-response.

======================================================================
5. VERSIONED EVIDENCE INTERPRETATION
======================================================================

An EVIDENCE INTERPRETATION links one or more observations to one or more learning claims/KCs.

It records:
- inference rule/model version;
- mapping version;
- evidence channel/construct;
- validity/confound status;
- support conditions;
- whether evidence is comparable with other evidence channels;
- uncertainty/quality reason.

CONFLICT RULE
Do not arbitrarily average contradictory observations.

If recognition is strong but application is weak, preserve the difference.
If evidence conflicts, uncertainty widens or the state becomes CONFLICTING EVIDENCE.
Only an explicit versioned inference model may combine channels.

======================================================================
6. LEARNER ESTIMATE — DERIVED, TIME-BOUND, UNCERTAIN
======================================================================

StudyGrid does not store “learner truth.”
It computes DERIVED LEARNER ESTIMATES for learning claims/KCs.

Minimum V1 estimate should be simple and inspectable:
- evidence history by channel;
- current/delayed performance summaries;
- recency;
- task-family diversity;
- support/coaching context;
- contradiction state;
- uncertainty reason;
- model/version/time.

DO NOT START V1 WITH BKT + PFA + IRT + CDM + FSRS + deep KT + bandits.

V1 should begin with a conservative, channel-aware estimate and optionally ONE interpretable baseline model where the data structure genuinely fits.

UNCERTAINTY REASON CODES
At minimum:
- UNMEASURED;
- SPARSE;
- CONFLICTING;
- INVALID_OR_LOW_QUALITY;
- MAPPING_OR_SOURCE_UNCERTAIN;
- MODEL_UNSUPPORTED_OR_OUT_OF_DOMAIN.

NO FAKE PRECISION
Do not expose “83% mastered” merely because a model can output 0.83.

======================================================================
7. EXPOSED / DEMONSTRATED / MEMORY
======================================================================

Keep the semantic distinction, change the data model.

EXPOSED
Derived from observed instructional exposure. Exposure is NOT competence.

DEMONSTRATED
A bounded claim:
“Learner demonstrated X under Y conditions at time T, based on evidence E, with uncertainty U.”

RETENTION / MEMORY
There is NO universal durable `retained=true` field.

Where the construct supports a memory model, StudyGrid may maintain a time-indexed retrievability/stability estimate informed by delayed evidence.
For procedural, transfer-heavy or physical skills, delayed authentic performance may matter more than a recall curve.

If the model does not fit the construct, abstain from pretending it does.

======================================================================
8. MISCONCEPTION HYPOTHESES
======================================================================

A suspected misconception is its own versioned hypothesis, not a learner fact.

It must record:
- supporting observations;
- competing explanations;
- confidence/uncertainty;
- source/rubric/version;
- whether deterministic rubric evidence or AI-assisted classification produced it.

A misconception hypothesis may influence candidate actions, but it must not create a self-confirming loop.

======================================================================
9. CONTEXT AND POLICY ARE SEPARATE FROM LEARNER STATE
======================================================================

CONTEXT FACTS
- course/syllabus position;
- source/content versions;
- actual assessment/deadline facts;
- learner-declared goal/priority;
- recent study/recommendation history;
- optional current time/effort constraint;
- available content/tasks.

Time is OPTIONAL CONTEXT, not the center of the product.

ASSESSMENT / ASSISTANCE POLICY
Do not use one overloaded “activity mode” enum.
Track independent dimensions:
- assessment purpose;
- stakes/consequence;
- assistance policy;
- feedback timing/policy;
- scoring policy;
- accessibility/accommodation policy.

PRACTICE
Adaptive teaching/help can be allowed.

DIAGNOSTIC
Assistance may be restricted/delayed to preserve measurement.

GRADED / PROTECTED ASSESSMENT
No coaching that invalidates the assessment. Authorized accessibility/accommodation support remains separate from generic tutoring help.

======================================================================
10. PEDAGOGICAL ACTION MODEL
======================================================================

StudyGrid chooses among pedagogical actions, not just questions.

Core action families:
- TEACH;
- EXPLAIN;
- WORKED_EXAMPLE;
- GUIDED_PRACTICE;
- RETRIEVE;
- REVIEW;
- REMEDIATE;
- ASSESS;
- ADVANCE;
- PAUSE / COMPLETE.

These are PEDAGOGICAL ACTION TYPES.
They are separate from:
- the exact task/content instance;
- navigation/session control;
- scoring state;
- UI presentation.

======================================================================
11. NEXT-ACTION POLICY — PROPRIETARY CENTER
======================================================================

The proprietary center is not one magic model.
It is the versioned decision policy that combines the course model, learner evidence, uncertainty, retention, policy constraints and current context into a defensible next action.

V1 SHOULD NOT USE A FAKE-PRECISE “EXPECTED LEARNING VALUE” NUMBER.

V1 POLICY SHAPE

STEP 1 — GENERATE CANDIDATES
Produce possible actions that are actually available for the learner/course/context.

STEP 2 — APPLY HARD CONSTRAINTS
Examples:
- protected assessment forbids coaching actions not allowed by policy;
- unavailable/inaccessible content cannot be recommended;
- generated/unapproved course assertions cannot silently support canonical instruction;
- item/task must have an explicit current claim mapping;
- hard prerequisite rule applies only when its authority/status justifies it;
- avoid immediate identical-item repetition used merely to inflate evidence;
- learner privacy/opt-out/access rules must be respected.

STEP 3 — APPLY SMALL TRANSPARENT PRIORITY RULES
The exact order is a V1 hypothesis and must be testable. Candidate factors may include:
- required prerequisite/readiness gap;
- whether material has actually been introduced;
- repeated valid misconception/error evidence;
- evidence uncertainty/undercoverage;
- delayed review/retention risk where valid;
- real assessment/course relevance/urgency;
- learner-declared goal;
- recent repetition/diversity;
- optional effort/time as a tie-breaker/constraint.

Do NOT call these factors causal benefits unless evidence supports that claim.

STEP 4 — SELECT ONE PRIMARY ACTION
If one action is clearly preferred under the current rules, select it.
If several are similarly defensible, use transparent tie-breakers rather than pretending certainty.

STEP 5 — PRODUCE REASON TRACE
Record:
- candidate actions;
- exclusions and reasons;
- learner-estimate snapshot/version;
- relevant evidence references;
- context/policy facts;
- rule/model/config versions;
- selected action;
- uncertainty reason;
- alternatives if applicable.

STEP 6 — ALLOW LEARNER OVERRIDE
Override is agency, not evidence of mastery, motivation, laziness or disengagement.

STEP 7 — OBSERVE OUTCOME
The resulting learner interaction enters the observation ledger. It does not retroactively prove the original recommendation was causally optimal.

======================================================================
12. LOW-STAKES STRUGGLE / NUDGE CONTRACT
======================================================================

OWNER INTENT
During low-stakes practice/quiz work, StudyGrid may notice a real pattern of struggle and quietly offer a way out or a better learning action without making the learner feel bad.

DRAFT-1 PRODUCT CONTRACT
- non-blocking;
- inline where possible;
- easy to ignore;
- neutral, non-shaming language;
- no “you failed” framing;
- one useful offer, not repeated nagging;
- learner can keep going;
- support action is explicit: example, explanation, guided practice, prerequisite review, later review, etc.

POSSIBLE SURFACE
“This looks worth another angle.”
[Show an example] [Break it down] [Keep going]

Exact copy is not approved; this demonstrates the interaction contract.

DO NOT TRIGGER CORE ADAPTATION FROM
- hover time alone;
- rapid deletion/retyping alone;
- dwell time alone;
- pointer movement;
- generic “took too long” thresholds.

Those signals are too confounded for learner-state authority in V1.

ASSESSMENT SHIELD
When policy says the activity is protected/graded, coaching actions disappear or are disabled according to policy. The UI can make this contract visually obvious, but backend policy is authoritative.

======================================================================
13. LEARNER AGENCY — HARD PRODUCT/POLICY INVARIANT
======================================================================

StudyGrid should normally show:
- ONE strongest recommended action;
- a short reason;
- low-emphasis alternatives where useful;
- a simple escape/override.

The learner should not have to design the whole study plan.
But they must be able to escape a misguided or repetitive recommendation.

OVERRIDE RULE
An override does NOT directly update mastery, motivation, engagement or instructor risk state.
Optional override reason, if collected, is user-declared context and purpose-limited.

======================================================================
14. PRODUCT SHELL IMPLICATION — SOL + GEMINI RECONCILIATION
======================================================================

Engine v1 does not dictate all navigation, but the strongest current product synthesis is:

PRODUCT SKELETON
- durable signed-in Home/current workspace;
- class and personal study are both supported contexts;
- one selected context strip/selector;
- shallow context destinations such as Study / Materials / Progress;
- bounded guided study journey inside Study;
- optional source/material drawer rather than a second permanent rail;
- one recommended next action with observable reason;
- stable task/action geometry;
- explicit Study vs Teach context where roles differ.

ADAPTIVE HELP VISUAL DIRECTION
Use quiet inline assistance by default.
Selective premium/spatial elements may add delight, but must not slow long study sessions or become the engine metaphor itself.
Full-chat/co-pilot UI is a bounded tutoring/explanation option, not the entire StudyGrid identity.

======================================================================
15. INSTRUCTOR EVIDENCE CONTRACT
======================================================================

Instructor output uses three layers:

1. OBSERVATION
What happened? How many learners/items? Which task families? What time/context? What source/version?

2. INTERPRETATION / HYPOTHESIS
What might explain it? How uncertain is that interpretation? What competing explanation exists, including bad item/content quality?

3. POSSIBLE ACTION
What could the instructor inspect or choose to do?

Do not automatically claim:
- this lecture caused weakness;
- this content is ineffective;
- a student is lazy/disengaged;
- a prerequisite caused downstream errors;
- the instructor caused an outcome.

With a small beta cohort, instructor analytics are descriptive and inspectable, not population inference.

PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS
If StudyGrid surfaces a defensible class issue, an optional instructor-controlled action bridge can be useful (for example inspect examples, open relevant material, draft an announcement). This must be validated with real instructors before broad build-out.

======================================================================
16. GENERATIVE AI BOUNDARY
======================================================================

Generative AI can assist with:
- proposed source parsing;
- proposed assertion/claim/KC mapping;
- draft explanations/examples/items;
- rubric-grounded constructed-response assistance;
- accessibility/wording variants;
- instructor evidence summaries.

Generative AI cannot silently become:
- canonical course truth;
- permanent learner truth;
- hidden prerequisite authority;
- sole high-stakes scorer without validated process;
- undocumented recommendation authority.

Any AI-generated/scored artifact that affects learner evidence must preserve:
- provider/model/version;
- relevant prompt/config or artifact hash/version;
- source/rubric references;
- validation/approval state;
- safe fallback when service is unavailable.

======================================================================
17. DATA DOMAINS / PRIVACY
======================================================================

Do not throw everything into one generic event stream.

A. LEARNING EVIDENCE
Purpose-limited educational record; provenance-rich; used for learner estimates and decision support.

B. OPERATIONAL / DEBUG TELEMETRY
Shorter retention/access-separated; used to diagnose software/accessibility failures; not learner-model evidence by default.

C. ACCESSIBILITY / ACCOMMODATION DECLARATIONS
Explicit user/institution-provided information where needed; restricted access; never inferred from interaction behavior.

D. OPTIONAL RESEARCH TELEMETRY
Separate governance/consent/purpose. It never silently becomes production learner state.

CORE ENGINE MUST STILL WORK IF OPTIONAL TELEMETRY IS DISABLED.

======================================================================
18. EXTERNAL / PHYSICAL EVIDENCE PATH
======================================================================

StudyGrid must not infer real-world procedural competence merely because a learner sequenced steps correctly on-screen.

V1 needs either:
- an explicit instructor/assessor external evidence pathway; or
- an abstention/insufficient-evidence state.

Screen-only proxies cannot silently become claims about physical execution or field performance.

======================================================================
19. REPLAYABILITY / VERSIONING
======================================================================

A material recommendation must be reconstructable later.

Version at minimum:
- source artifact;
- assertion/procedure;
- curriculum outcome/claim/KC;
- relation edge;
- task/item;
- scoring/rubric;
- task→claim mapping;
- evidence interpretation schema/model;
- learner-estimator model/config;
- next-action policy/config;
- generative/scoring model where relevant.

SOURCE REVISION CASCADE
When source/course truth changes, StudyGrid must be able to identify affected:
- assertions;
- tasks;
- mappings;
- evidence interpretations;
- learner estimates;
- prior recommendation explanations.

Historical observations remain historical facts. Derived interpretations may be invalidated/recomputed.

======================================================================
20. HARD INVARIANTS
======================================================================

Engine v1 should treat these as hard contracts unless explicitly revised:

1. Observations are immutable; interpretations are separate/versioned.
2. Source/assertion provenance and versions are preserved.
3. Generated AI output never silently becomes canonical course truth.
4. No task is evidence for a claim without an explicit versioned mapping.
5. Assessment/assistance policy is explicit; accessibility support is not generic tutoring.
6. Learner override is not mastery/motivation/disengagement evidence.
7. No behavior-based inference of disability, health, emotion, personality, intelligence or laziness.
8. Optional high-intrusion telemetry is not required for core adaptation and does not silently enter learner state.
9. Every material next-action decision records candidates/exclusions, selected action and policy/model/config versions.
10. Course/source/model/policy changes are versioned.
11. Insufficient/invalid/conflicting evidence can block a strong claim.
12. Exposure/completion is never mastery by itself.
13. Coached success is distinguishable from unaided evidence.
14. Repeated identical-item success is not automatically transfer.
15. Protected assessment cannot receive unauthorized coaching.

======================================================================
21. THINGS STUDYGRID SHOULD INTENTIONALLY REFUSE TO INFER
======================================================================

Absent explicit validated need + proper governance, do not infer:
- broad intelligence/aptitude/ability traits from ordinary course interactions;
- motivation/laziness/diligence/engagement from clicks, latency, overrides or absence;
- disability/health/emotion/personality/mental state from behavior;
- cheating from response time alone;
- durable competence from one correct answer;
- lack of competence from non-use/non-response;
- causal content effectiveness from cohort correlation;
- causal prerequisite bottlenecks from co-occurring errors;
- instructor quality from learner outcomes;
- demographic/sensitive traits from behavioral traces;
- offline learning state unless learner/instructor supplies evidence;
- physical/procedural competence from screen-only proxies without validated transfer evidence.

22. SMALLEST HONEST BETA ENGINE
======================================================================

For the first ~20-student + professor beta, build/design toward ONLY this coherent surface:

1. versioned sources + assertions;
2. curriculum outcomes / learning claims + typed relations;
3. domain-neutral task/item + scoring model;
4. explicit task→claim mappings;
5. immutable attempt/observation ledger;
6. versioned evidence interpretation;
7. simple transparent learner estimate with uncertainty;
8. explicit course/assessment/assistance policy;
9. small rule-based/lexicographic next-action policy;
10. recommendation reason trace/replay;
11. quiet adaptive-help UX in practice;
12. minimal instructor observation/hypothesis/action view;
13. sparse pedagogically meaningful instrumentation.

DO NOT BUILD YET JUST BECAUSE RESEARCH NAMED IT
- deep/transformer KT authority;
- reinforcement learning;
- contextual bandit policy optimization;
- generalized IRT/CDM stack everywhere;
- elaborate psychometric dashboards;
- microservices for model families;
- vector database solely because “AI”; 
- invasive behavior telemetry;
- giant instructor dashboard;
- full conversational shell as the product;
- fake AI-generated personalized course plans without evidence.

======================================================================
23. BETA PURPOSE / ACCEPTANCE
======================================================================

THE BETA CAN TEST
- whether the course/claim structure makes sense;
- whether item→claim mappings are believable;
- whether interaction types work;
- whether learner estimates avoid obvious nonsense;
- whether “Why this next?” explanations make sense;
- whether students trust/override recommendations appropriately;
- whether quiet remediation helps rather than annoys;
- whether StudyGrid can distinguish software/item problems from learner weakness;
- whether instructor summaries match real instructor decisions;
- whether data collection feels understandable and proportionate;
- whether replay/versioning works.

THE BETA CANNOT PROVE
- broad efficacy;
- long-term retention superiority;
- fairness across populations;
- one learner-model family is universally best;
- causal value of the full adaptive policy;
- institutional-scale reliability.

STOP / REVISIT CONDITIONS SHOULD BE PREDECLARED BEFORE BETA
Examples:
- repeated source/claim mapping errors;
- recommendations that contradict explicit course policy;
- inability to reconstruct why a recommendation happened;
- systematic conflation of UI/input failure with learner weakness;
- professor judges surfaced analytics misleading/unactionable;
- learners cannot understand why StudyGrid chose an action;
- privacy/telemetry surprises users;
- model becomes more confident when evidence becomes more contradictory.

Numeric thresholds are NOT set in Draft 1; they require beta protocol design.

======================================================================
24. MODEL CHALLENGER GATE
======================================================================

Any future more-complex learner model or learned ranking policy must compete against the simple interpretable baseline.

A challenger does NOT win merely because it has better next-response AUC/accuracy.

It must show incremental value on relevant dimensions such as:
- calibration;
- robustness across courses/cohorts;
- useful decision changes;
- delayed/transfer learning outcomes where measurable;
- fairness/accessibility behavior;
- interpretability/audit cost;
- operational reliability.

If complexity does not materially improve the learning decision, do not add it.

======================================================================
25. FALSIFICATION SUITE — FIRST TESTS TO WRITE BEFORE IMPLEMENTATION RELEASE
======================================================================

A. ONE CORRECT ANSWER
One easy correct answer must not create a durable competence claim.

B. RECOGNITION VS APPLICATION
Strong MCQ recognition + weak novel application must remain conflicting/channel-specific evidence.

C. COACHED VS UNAIDED
Correct after a worked example must not be treated identically to delayed unaided retrieval.

D. OLD SOURCE VERSION
A changed source assertion must allow affected tasks/mappings/interpretations to be identified without rewriting raw attempts.

E. BAD ITEM MAPPING
One deliberately wrong item→claim mapping must be detectable/reversible without rewriting learner history.

F. BAD PREREQUISITE EDGE
A soft/hypothesized edge must not permanently trap the learner as if it were an explicit hard prerequisite.

G. OVERRIDE
Ten learner overrides must not lower mastery or mark disengagement.

H. ACCESSIBILITY / LATENCY
Slow response under assistive-tech conditions must not automatically reduce learner competence.

I. LOW-STAKES NUDGE
Repeated valid struggle in practice may surface quiet help; learner can ignore it and continue.

J. PROTECTED TEST
Same evidence/context in a protected graded assessment must suppress unauthorized coaching.

K. CONFLICT
Conflicting high-quality evidence must increase uncertainty rather than average to a fake middle certainty.

L. SMALL-N INSTRUCTOR SIGNAL
One flawed item causing many misses should surface item-quality as a competing explanation, not automatically “class weakness.”

M. AI SCORER VERSION CHANGE
Historical scores must identify which scorer/model/version created them.

N. GENERATIVE AI OUTAGE
Core course truth, evidence history and next-action policy must remain operable with a safe degraded path.

O. OPTIONAL TELEMETRY OFF
Core adaptation must still function without hover/pointer/keystroke/research telemetry.

======================================================================
26. WHAT MAKES STUDYGRID STUDYGRID — DRAFT 1 FORMULATION
======================================================================

StudyGrid turns real learning material into a versioned map of what a learner may need to know or do, records what the learner actually does, interprets that evidence conservatively, and uses an inspectable policy to choose a defensible next teaching/practice/review action — while remembering uncertainty, course rules and what happened before.

The moat is not a quiz generator, one knowledge-tracing model, or an AI chat interface.

The core product loop is:
SOURCE/COURSE → CLAIMS/RELATIONS → TASK → OBSERVATION → EVIDENCE INTERPRETATION → LEARNER ESTIMATE → ELIGIBLE ACTIONS → POLICY DECISION → TEACH/PRACTICE/REVIEW → NEW OBSERVATION.

The user-facing experience should remain much simpler:
“Here’s what matters next. Here’s why. Let’s do it.”

11. How much of the product shell should expose source material during practice without leaking answers.
12. Engineering representation of versioning/replay without over-building event sourcing or ML infrastructure — explicitly reserved for Claude engineering-integrity review.
