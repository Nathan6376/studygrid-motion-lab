METHOD
Every primitive below is provisional. For each: CLAIM -> WHY -> EVIDENCE/REASON -> STRONGEST ATTACK -> FAILURE CASE -> REVISED RULE -> TEST.
The engine should prefer explicit uncertainty over false precision. Prediction quality is not equivalent to teaching quality.

======================================================================
0. PRODUCT PURPOSE
======================================================================
CLAIM
StudyGrid exists to help a learner make better learning progress by choosing and delivering the next pedagogically useful action from their actual course/context, while preserving inspectable evidence and teacher usefulness.

WHY
A collection of notes, quizzes, flashcards or AI chat is replaceable. The durable system is the loop that interprets course structure + learner evidence and decides what to do next.

STRONGEST ATTACK
“Next best action” can become paternalistic, overconfident or optimized for the wrong metric.

REVISED RULE
StudyGrid recommends one primary next action with a reason, preserves learner override, and records uncertainty. It must not equate engagement, completion, streaks or short-term accuracy with durable learning.

TEST
Can the system explain what it recommended, why, what evidence it used, what uncertainty remains, and what would have changed the recommendation?

======================================================================
1. PRIMITIVE: LEARNING TARGET
======================================================================
CLAIM
The atomic thing StudyGrid reasons about should be a LEARNING TARGET, not merely a chapter/topic/concept label.

DEFINITION
A Learning Target is a versioned, source-grounded statement of something a learner may need to know, understand, distinguish, explain, recall, perform, calculate, sequence, diagnose or apply.

EXAMPLES
- “Distinguish X from Y under condition Z.”
- “Apply formula F to solve this class of problem.”
- “Sequence the steps of procedure P.”
- “Explain principle Q in plain language.”

WHY
“Concept” is too narrow for procedures, calculations, discrimination, explanation and applied skills. “Chapter 4” is too broad to support evidence or next-action decisions.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Over-atomizing knowledge creates a brittle graph with thousands of tiny targets and fake precision.

FAILURE CASE
A system splits every sentence into a target and then claims mastery of micro-fragments that do not transfer to real performance.

REVISED RULE
A Learning Target must be small enough to support a meaningful evidence claim but large enough to represent a coherent educational capability. Targets may have subtargets and multiple forms of evidence. Granularity must be validated by teaching/assessment usefulness, not token count.

TEST
For a proposed target, can we answer: (a) what would count as evidence, (b) what would not count, (c) what downstream action changes if this target is weak/strong, and (d) can an instructor recognize it as pedagogically meaningful?

======================================================================
2. PRIMITIVE: SOURCE / COURSE TRUTH
======================================================================
CLAIM
Every Learning Target and every material teaching/assessment claim should trace to versioned authoritative source material or an explicitly approved instructor/course assertion.

WHY
The engine cannot be trustworthy if generated content silently becomes course truth.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Some learning targets are implicit across multiple sources, inferred from assessment patterns, or represent general prerequisite knowledge not stated verbatim.

REVISED RULE
StudyGrid distinguishes AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE, INSTRUCTOR-APPROVED ASSERTION, SYSTEM-INFERRED CANDIDATE, and EXTERNAL REFERENCE. Inferred candidates may help authoring but do not silently become canonical.

TEST
For any target/question/explanation, can the system show its provenance and version, or clearly state that it is an unapproved candidate?

======================================================================
3. PRIMITIVE: EVIDENCE EVENT
======================================================================
CLAIM
StudyGrid should never update learner state from “activity” in the abstract. It updates from typed EVIDENCE EVENTS.

DEFINITION
An Evidence Event is an observed learner interaction that may say something about one or more Learning Targets, with explicit provenance, interaction type, scoring/result, evidence quality and context.

CORE EXAMPLES
- answer committed + score/partial score
- misconception/rubric category when valid
- confidence supplied before feedback (optional)
- retrieval after a delay
- guided step completed with/without support
- learner requested explanation/hint (context only, not automatic mastery penalty)

NOT MASTERY EVIDENCE BY ITSELF
- page viewed
- lesson opened
- time on page
- scrolling
- streak
- completion checkbox

WHY
Exposure and engagement are not the same as learning.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Even correctness is noisy: guessing, bad questions, repeated item memorization, accessibility/device issues, ambiguous wording.

REVISED RULE
Every Evidence Event carries an evidence-quality/diagnostic-weight concept. No single event equals “learned.” The engine may mark evidence as weak, contradictory, low-quality or insufficient rather than forcing a state update.

TEST
Can a reviewer see why a particular event changed learner state, by how much/what direction, and what confounds were considered?

======================================================================
4. PRIMITIVE: LEARNER STATE IS AN ESTIMATE, NOT TRUTH
======================================================================
CLAIM
StudyGrid should maintain an evidence-based estimate for each Learning Target, with uncertainty and instructional state, rather than a single fake mastery percentage.

MINIMUM DIMENSIONS / STATES TO EXPLORE
- exposure/introduction state: not introduced vs introduced
- current performance evidence
- retention/stability evidence over time
- uncertainty / amount-quality of evidence
- misconception suspicion / competing explanations
- instructional state: needs teaching, guided practice, retrieval, review, remediation, or ready to advance

WHY
A learner can understand now but not retain later; can answer correctly with weak confidence; can have sparse evidence; can show a specific misconception rather than generic weakness.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Too many state dimensions create complexity that is impossible to calibrate or explain.

REVISED RULE
Separate only dimensions that can change a pedagogical decision. If two state fields never change what StudyGrid does, merge/remove them.

TEST
For each state field: name at least one decision that changes because of it and one observed event that validly updates it.

======================================================================
5. DISTINCTION: LEARNED VS RETAINED VS EXPOSED
======================================================================
CLAIM
StudyGrid must distinguish at least:
- EXPOSED: learner encountered the material;
- LEARNED/CURRENTLY DEMONSTRATED: enough current evidence supports competent performance on the intended construct;
- RETAINED: later/delayed evidence supports that the learning remains retrievable/usable over time.

WHY
A correct answer immediately after instruction is not evidence of durable retention. A page view is not evidence of understanding.

STRONGEST ATTACK
“Learned” is construct-dependent; there is no universal threshold across recalling a definition, solving an equation and applying judgment in a scenario.

REVISED RULE
“Learned” is not one global threshold. Each Learning Target has an EVIDENCE CONTRACT describing what forms/coverage of evidence can support the claim. Retention requires temporally separated evidence appropriate to the target.

TEST
Can two targets use different evidence contracts without changing the engine core? Can the system refuse to claim learned/retained when the contract is unmet?

======================================================================
6. PRIMITIVE: EVIDENCE CONTRACT / CONSTRUCT ALIGNMENT
======================================================================
CLAIM
Each Learning Target needs an Evidence Contract specifying what kind of performance actually demonstrates it.

POSSIBLE CONSTRUCT TYPES
- recognition / discrimination
- recall
- sequence/order
- calculation
- explanation
- application/scenario
- diagnosis/classification
- procedure/performance
- synthesis/constructed response

WHY
Multiple choice may test recognition when the course requires production or application. The interaction must match the capability.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Real competencies often require several constructs and cannot be captured by one interaction.

REVISED RULE
Evidence Contracts may require a portfolio of evidence types and can map one event to multiple targets with explicit weights/roles.

TEST
Would passing the Evidence Contract convince a knowledgeable instructor that the learner can do what the target claims? If not, the contract is invalid.

======================================================================
7. PRIMITIVE: ACTIVITY / INTERVENTION
======================================================================
CLAIM
StudyGrid chooses among pedagogical actions, not just questions.

ACTION FAMILIES
TEACH, EXPLAIN, WORKED_EXAMPLE, GUIDED_PRACTICE, RETRIEVE, REVIEW, REMEDIATE, ASSESS, ADVANCE, PAUSE/COMPLETE.

WHY
When evidence suggests missing prerequisite knowledge or misunderstanding, another quiz item may be the wrong intervention.

STRONGEST ATTACK
A rules engine can overreact to one wrong answer and constantly interrupt the learner.

REVISED RULE
Interventions are selected using evidence quality, pattern/history, uncertainty and activity mode. The system should generally prefer low-friction support and allow learner control unless a bounded flow requires otherwise.

TEST
Construct cases where the same wrong answer leads to different actions because context differs: first exposure, repeated error, high-confidence misconception, graded assessment, practice session, retention review.

======================================================================
8. PRIMITIVE: ACTIVITY MODE / INTERVENTION PERMISSION
======================================================================
CLAIM
The engine must know what kind of activity is occurring before it decides whether intervention is allowed.

MINIMUM MODES TO DESIGN
- LEARNING/PRACTICE: adaptive teaching/nudges allowed.
- DIAGNOSTIC: support may be delayed/restricted to preserve measurement.
- GRADED/HIGH-STAKES ASSESSMENT: no coaching that invalidates assessment; only permitted accessibility/operational help.
- REVIEW/SELF-TEST: configurable balance between authentic testing and teach-on-error.

OWNER EXAMPLE
During a low-stakes quiz, if repeated evidence indicates struggle, StudyGrid may offer a non-shaming option such as “This topic looks shaky. Review it now / keep going,” while a real graded test may forbid that intervention.

WHY
An intervention useful in practice can invalidate an assessment.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Mode labels can be wrong or instructor policy can differ.

REVISED RULE
Mode is explicit and policy-governed. Instructor/course policy can further constrain assistance. The engine never silently downgrades assessment integrity for adaptivity.

TEST
For the same learner state and same item, verify the engine produces different allowable action sets in practice vs diagnostic vs graded modes.

======================================================================
9. PRIMITIVE: CONTEXT
======================================================================
CLAIM
Next-action decisions require context beyond learner state.

CORE CONTEXT
- course/syllabus position
- explicit course outcomes and source versions
- upcoming assessment/deadline relevance
- prerequisite graph
- learner goal if explicitly supplied
- available/allowed activity types
- recent recommendation/activity history
- optional time/effort constraint

WHY
A weak peripheral concept due in three months should not always outrank a foundational prerequisite blocking tomorrow’s assessment.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Urgency can turn StudyGrid into a cramming optimizer and distort long-term learning.

REVISED RULE
Assessment urgency is a priority signal, not sole authority. Policy should balance readiness, foundational importance, retention and near-term relevance.

TEST
Create conflicting cases (urgent-but-foundationally-blocked, weak-but-low-value, due-for-review-but-new-prerequisite) and require an inspectable rationale.

======================================================================
10. PRIMITIVE: NEXT-ACTION POLICY
======================================================================
CLAIM
The proprietary center is a policy that generates eligible actions, applies hard constraints, ranks them with interpretable factors, returns one primary recommendation plus optional alternatives, and records a reason trace.

V1 FACTOR FAMILIES TO TEST
- prerequisite/readiness eligibility
- expected learning value
- evidence weakness/uncertainty
- repeated misconception severity
- retention risk
- assessment/course importance/urgency
- undercoverage
- learner/course position
- anti-repetition/diversity
- instructional-state constraint
- optional effort/time cost

WHY
This is where StudyGrid combines otherwise-available components into a coherent learning system.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Hand-designed weights can encode arbitrary assumptions and look scientific without causal proof.

REVISED RULE
V1 weights/thresholds are explicit hypotheses, versioned and testable. Hard semantic/safety rules are separated from tunable ranking. Learned ranking is introduced only when data can validate it against an interpretable baseline and learning outcomes.

TEST
For every recommendation, store eligible candidates, excluded candidates/reasons, factor values, policy version, winner and uncertainty. Replay should reproduce the decision.

======================================================================
11. PRIMITIVE: REASON TRACE / REPLAYABILITY
======================================================================
CLAIM
Every material adaptive decision must be replayable and explainable internally.

MINIMUM TRACE
- learner-state snapshot/version
- relevant evidence window
- source/course version
- candidate actions
- hard constraints applied
- policy/model versions
- reason factors
- selected action
- uncertainty/abstention state

WHY
Without this, StudyGrid cannot debug, scientifically improve, defend institutional claims or investigate harmful recommendations.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Full traces can become privacy-heavy and expensive.

REVISED RULE
Store the minimum structured reason trace needed for reproducibility; do not store invasive raw telemetry merely because it might help explain behavior.

TEST
Take a historical recommendation and reproduce why it happened without relying on an LLM’s memory or undocumented code path.

======================================================================
12. PRIMITIVE: ABSTAIN / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE
======================================================================
CLAIM
“Insufficient evidence” is a valid engine output.

WHY
Cold start and noisy evidence make forced personalization dangerous.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Too much abstention makes StudyGrid feel dumb.

REVISED RULE
When uncertainty is high, choose actions that are useful under many plausible learner states and have information value without turning the learner into a test subject. The UI need not say “algorithm uncertain” every time, but the engine must preserve that state.

TEST
On a brand-new learner/course, verify that the system does not fabricate precise mastery and can still recommend useful introductory/diagnostic actions.

======================================================================
13. PRIMITIVE: LEARNER AGENCY
======================================================================
CLAIM
The recommendation is strong guidance, not absolute control.

WHY
The learner may know context StudyGrid cannot observe (offline studying, instructor hints, personal priorities), and over-control can create algorithmic traps.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Too many overrides recreate the original problem of forcing the learner to design the study plan.

REVISED RULE
Show one clear default next action. Offer low-emphasis alternatives/override when useful. Learn from override reasons cautiously; do not treat override as evidence of mastery or disengagement.

TEST
Can learners escape a repetitive/misguided recommendation without navigating a settings maze? Does the engine avoid punishing the override?

======================================================================
14. PRIMITIVE: INSTRUCTOR EVIDENCE, NOT SURVEILLANCE
======================================================================
CLAIM
Instructor analytics should summarize learning evidence and decision-relevant uncertainty, not behavioral surveillance.

POTENTIAL SIGNALS
- concepts with repeated error after sufficient exposure
- common misconception categories
- prerequisite bottlenecks
- unusual item/content confusion
- coverage gaps
- insufficient-evidence students/concepts
- retention review candidates

WHY
Professors need useful decisions, not another dashboard full of clicks and dwell time.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Aggregates can imply causality or stigmatize learners.

REVISED RULE
Every surfaced instructor insight separates observation from interpretation/recommendation, includes sample/uncertainty, and allows inspection of underlying pedagogical evidence. Do not infer motivation, laziness, intelligence, disability or personality.

TEST
For each insight ask: What exactly was observed? How many learners/items? What uncertainty? What action could an instructor reasonably consider? What claim would be an overreach?

======================================================================
15. AI BOUNDARY
======================================================================
CLAIM
Generative AI assists content understanding/generation/explanation but does not own course truth, learner truth or core decision state.

SAFE CANDIDATE ROLES
- propose target extraction/tagging from sources
- draft explanations/examples/question variants
- rubric-grounded free-text assistance
- summarize instructor evidence with provenance
- create accessibility/wording variants

NOT SOLE AUTHORITY FOR
- canonical course facts
- silent graph mutations
- permanent mastery updates
- high-stakes instructor conclusions
- unlogged next-action decisions

WHY
LLMs are probabilistic and can hallucinate or drift; core state requires reproducibility and auditability.

TEST
Can the engine continue operating deterministically with generative AI unavailable, albeit with fewer generated materials? Can all AI outputs be traced to source/model/version and approval status?

======================================================================
16. DATA MINIMIZATION
======================================================================
CLAIM
StudyGrid should collect pedagogically meaningful events, not everything technically observable.

DEFAULT AVOID
raw cursor paths, keystroke dynamics, continuous screen recording, background-window surveillance, inferred emotion/personality/health/disability.

WHY
These signals are noisy, accessibility-confounded and privacy-expensive.

STRONGEST ATTACK
Some fine-grained telemetry may improve prediction.

REVISED RULE
Prediction gain alone is insufficient. A new signal must demonstrate incremental pedagogical value, acceptable privacy/fairness cost, and a legitimate use purpose before adoption.

TEST
For every proposed signal, require: intended decision improved, evidence it helps, confounds, privacy cost, retention policy, user/institution value, and lower-intrusion alternatives.

======================================================================
17. V1 SUCCESS DEFINITION
======================================================================
CLAIM
Engine v1 succeeds if it is coherent, inspectable, useful and falsifiable—not if it appears maximally “AI.”

EARLY SUCCESS CRITERIA
- course/target/source model is internally consistent and reviewable;
- learner state never claims more certainty than evidence supports;
- recommendation reason traces are complete/replayable;
- teaching vs testing interventions follow mode/construct rules;
- students can understand Up Next and recover from wrong recommendations;
- instructor evidence is actionable enough to validate with real professors;
- baseline model can be compared against future statistical/deep challengers;
- beta exposes wrong assumptions without corrupting durable truth.

NON-GOALS FOR ~20-PERSON BETA
- proving broad efficacy
- training a deep adaptive model
- proving fairness across populations
- proving institutional-scale reliability
- declaring one learner-state model universally best

18. REQUIRED ADVERSARIAL REVIEW QUESTIONS
======================================================================
A reviewer must actively try to disprove this draft:
1. Is “Learning Target” actually the right atomic primitive, or is another ontology better?
2. Which distinctions above are redundant or falsely precise?
3. Which required evidence would be impossible to validate across domains?
4. What breaks in mathematics/engineering vs law/forensics vs memorization-heavy material?
5. Where could an instructor-authored bad graph poison the engine?
6. How could a learner game the evidence model?
7. Which state labels risk becoming circular (“needs teaching because state says needs teaching”)?
8. Can the next-action policy accidentally optimize short-term accuracy over durable learning?
9. How should conflicting evidence be represented without arbitrary averaging?
10. Which rules need hard invariants vs tunable hypotheses?
11. What is missing for accessibility, accommodations, language, or alternative response modalities?
12. What should StudyGrid intentionally NOT infer?
13. What would make this architecture expensive/impossible to operate at institutional scale?
14. Which parts duplicate proven standards/libraries unnecessarily?
15. What is the smallest coherent Engine v1 that could be beta-tested without pretending to be more intelligent than it is?
